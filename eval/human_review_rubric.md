# Human review: relevance and tone (M3 evaluation plan, method 3)

Two teammates each fill in their **own copy** of the sheet:
blank sheets (completed copies: `eval/Human_Review_1.csv` and `eval/Human_Review_2.csv`). Don't look at the other rater's scores until you're both done.

**What's being rated:** 32 bullets SignalBrief published on 17 Nov 2026:
- the full E9 newsletter (24 bullets, 9 topics; the cancer-research topic published none);
- all 8 bullets from the three E3 research runs (needed for E3's "framed as single studies" check).

For each bullet, read the topic and **why the reader cares** first, then the bullet. Rate it as the reader would see it in their inbox. The cited headline is there for context only. Accuracy against the headline was already checked in E10, so it doesn't count here.

## Relevance (1-5): would *this* reader want this in their brief?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Exactly what the reader asked to follow (a launch, release, study or event squarely on the topic and the "why") |
| 4 | Clearly on topic and useful, but not the most important kind of update |
| 3 | On topic but marginal: minor, old news, or only loosely tied to the "why" |
| 2 | Barely related: shares a keyword but not what the reader cares about |
| 1 | Off topic, or something the reader said they don't want (e.g. a sale or deal) |

## Tone (1-5): is it written the way a careful newsletter should be?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Plain, neutral and clear; one quick read is enough |
| 4 | Fine, with one small issue (a bit wordy, one unexplained term) |
| 3 | Noticeable problem: jargon a general reader wouldn't know, awkward, or mild hype |
| 2 | Hype or clickbait wording, or hard to follow |
| 1 | Sensational or misleading in tone (e.g. "miracle", "cure", "game-changer") |

## Single-study framing (research bullets only): y / n

**y** = the bullet presents the finding as one study's result (e.g. "a phase 2 trial found…", "researchers report…", "a review outlines…").
**n** = it states the finding as settled fact, or implies a proven treatment.
Bullets from news and artist topics are marked `n/a`; leave them.

## What we report

- Mean relevance and mean tone per rater, and overall.
- Agreement between raters: share of bullets where the two scores are within 1 point.
- E3 human check: passes if every E3 research bullet is rated **y** by both raters.

Use `notes` for anything worth quoting in the report (e.g. "duplicate of #3", "wrong topic").
